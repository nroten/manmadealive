▲ The formation gap

Most churches form men through Sunday attendance alone.

The Lewis Center for Church Leadership (2024) calls this the collapse of "faith formation by osmosis" — the assumption that men sitting in pews are being formed through worship and preaching alone. In a post-pandemic environment, most highly committed attendees are in church one to two Sundays per month. That assumption is no longer sufficient.

The man showing up faithfully in your congregation is almost certainly carrying real responsibility without real formation. He is not lazy. He is not faithless. He is a good man with insufficient tools.

◆ What the research confirms

These men haven't abandoned faith — they're hungry for something more.

Men's church attendance is rising — Barna's 2025 data shows men now outpace women (43% vs 36%), the first time in recorded survey history. Bible use among men climbed 19% in a single year. Commitment to Christ among Millennial men has risen 19 points since 2019.

"He is not a man who has abandoned his faith. He is a man who has been faithful with insufficient tools."

Why other approaches fall short

Every program lands a man
somewhere in the overlaps.

Walk through the Venn verbally. Each overlap names something the pastor has probably watched happen in his own men's ministry. Let him make the connections himself.

◆ The insight

Every men's program offers at least one of these three. None alone, or in pairs, produces lasting formation.

Knowledge without vision and brotherhood produces informed but unchanged men. Inspiration without foundation and accountability produces the conference high — gone by Thursday. Accountability without truth or passion produces performance and shame.

Even effective combinations of two fall short. The conference curriculum moves and teaches — but without brothers to sustain it, the change fades. The accountability group structures and holds — but without the theological foundation of truth, the direction is often wrong.

"Every program a man has tried landed him somewhere in the overlaps. MMA exists to bring him to the center."

◆ The biblical framework

Information changes what a man knows. Formation changes who a man is.

For lasting change to take root, truth must travel the full distance — from the mind, through the heart, and out through the hands. That journey is what formation is for.

Most programs reach the head. Some reach the heart. Almost none stay long enough to reach the hands.

Man Made Alive is built for the full journey. The five modules are not arbitrary — they follow the exact sequence that Scripture describes and that formation actually requires.

The transformation is the Spirit's work. The formation pathway is the structured environment in which a man cooperates with that work — through truth, through training, through brotherhood sustained over time.

"Most programs reach the head. Some reach the heart. Almost none stay long enough to reach the hands."

◆ Man Made Alive · A Conversation for Pastors 05 / 08
What MMA is — and refuses to be

The theological convictions
and the five-stage pathway.

This is the vetting page. A pastor needs to know the theological convictions before he can recommend this to his men. State them directly. Don't soften them.

◆ The theological convictions

These are not positions. They are the load-bearing walls of everything MMA is built on.

Christ-Centered
Jesus is not a role model among options. He is the pattern — the only complete picture of what strength, authority, gentleness, and submission look like rightly ordered. The goal is formation into the likeness of Christ.
Scripture-Anchored
Scripture is the authority. Formation tools serve — they do not replace. The charter, the content, the community are structured ways to walk more faithfully in the direction Scripture points.
Complementarian
MMA holds a complementarian view of manhood — not polemically, but as the theological foundation from which the entire vision of responsibility and strength is built.
Grounded in Historical Biblical Doctrine
Transformation is God's work. Formation is a man's Spirit-empowered cooperation with that work. Men do not train to earn life — they train in response to life already given in Christ (Ephesians 2:4–5).
→ The five-stage pathway

Each stage builds on the last in an order that cannot be reversed without weakening what follows.

01 · TRUTH
Lifting the fog.
Name the drift. Expose cultural distortions. Anchor in the pattern of Christ.
02 · TRAINING
From trying to training.
Build capacity through rhythm. Discipline as alignment, not punishment.
03 · TACTICS
Where manhood is lived.
Four arenas of responsibility. Strength aimed outward, faithfully.
04 · TRACTION
Faithfulness over time.
Brotherhood. Honest accountability. Momentum that doesn't depend on motivation.
05 · TRIUMPH
The long arc.
Legacy. Mission. A life aimed outward for the glory of God.

MMA does not compete with the local church.

◆ Man Made Alive · A Conversation for Pastors 06 / 08
The man sitting in your congregation

Not a demographic.
A person you already know.

Ask the pastor to think of three specific men while you walk through this. The goal is for him to see his people — not a category.

◆ Who he is

He is between 28 and 55. Probably married, likely a father. He attends your church regularly — perhaps serving in some capacity. He works. He provides. He carries real weight. He is not lazy, not faithless, not checked out. By almost every external measure, he is a good man.

And yet something feels persistently, quietly off. There is a tension between who he senses he is supposed to be and how he is actually living. He carries it without naming it. He suspects he is the only one who feels it. He is not.

"He is a man who is ready to engage a process that is honest about what formation actually requires — and serious enough to ask something real of him."

— Man Made Alive
▲ What he is carrying
In his marriage
He loves his wife and is often uncertain how to lead her. He avoids conflict because conflict exhausts him. Important things go unsaid for months and surface wrong.
As a father
He knows the window with his children is not unlimited. He knows his presence matters. He does not know how to close the gap between his intentions and his actual impact.
In his friendships
He likely has men he watches football with. He almost certainly does not have a man who knows enough of his story to notice when he starts drifting — and say something about it.
In his faith
He is showing up. He is sincere. And he suspects that what he has been calling faith has mostly been attendance — and he does not know how to make it more than that.

How you can be part of this

Three tiers. Every pastor
chooses his own entry point.

Present the tiers in order and let him land where he's ready. The first tier is a one-sentence yes. Don't pressure toward the third.

Tier 01 · Lowest barrier

Point your men here.

The ask: one sentence — in a sermon, an email, or a bulletin.

Share the free three-video series with your men — no cost, no obligation, no formal partnership required. Simply tell your men: "There is a free resource I want you to look at." That is the entire first step. Many pastors begin here and go deeper as they see the fruit.

Tier 02 · Active partner

Champion it to your men.

The ask: a personal endorsement and ongoing promotion.

Personally recommend MMA to the men in your congregation — from the pulpit, in small groups, in pastoral conversations. Provide the free videos and charter as a church-wide resource. Receive regular updates on how your men are engaging the pathway.

Tier 03 · Church partnership

Integrate MMA into your church.

The ask: a formal, ongoing partnership.

Integrate MMA into the discipleship infrastructure of your church — a structured formation pathway for men that complements your preaching and small-group ministry. Group pricing available. Nate available to speak, lead a workshop, or train small group leaders on the formation framework.

Every tier begins with the same first step:
put the free video series in front of your men.

A final word

What I could not find was a formation pathway that was honest about the real problem, patient enough to work from the inside out, and serious enough to ask something real of a man.

The men in your congregation deserve that. Not a weekend retreat they will forget by Thursday. Not an accountability group that produces performance rather than formation. A pathway — one that works at the pace formation actually requires, walks in the order it demands, and holds because it is carried by brothers rather than sustained by willpower alone.
